Aisha Bello brings an unfair dismissal and discrimination claim in the employment tribunal in England. She instructs Marlow Achterberg, an SRA-authorised firm, under a damages-based agreement made in 2025. The tribunal awards her £60,000, all of which she recovers from her former employer. The agreement provides for the firm's payment to be the maximum percentage of her recovery permitted by law.

What is the maximum payment the firm may receive from Aisha under the damages-based agreement?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer B: £21,000, being 35% of the sum recovered, including VAT.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

In an employment matter, a damages-based agreement must not provide for a payment above 35% of the sums ultimately recovered, including VAT (DBA Regulations 2013 reg 7).

Damages-based agreements in employment claims: the 35% cap including VAT (DBA Regs reg 7)

The Damages-Based Agreements Regulations 2013 set caps on the payment a representative may take from a client's recovery. For employment matters, regulation 7 provides that a damages-based agreement must not provide for a payment above an amount which, including VAT, is equal to 35% of the sums ultimately recovered by the client. Aisha recovered £60,000, so the maximum payment is 35% of £60,000, which is £21,000, and that figure includes any VAT. The 25% cap in regulation 4(2) applies to personal injury claims, and the 50% cap in regulation 4(3) to other civil claims at first instance; neither applies to an employment matter.

Why A is wrong: 25% is the cap for personal injury claims under reg 4(2), not employment matters.

Why C is wrong: The 35% cap is inclusive of VAT, so VAT cannot be added on top.

Why D is wrong: The 50% cap in reg 4(3) applies to other civil claims, not employment matters.

Why E is wrong: DBAs are expressly permitted in employment matters, subject to the 35% cap in reg 7.
