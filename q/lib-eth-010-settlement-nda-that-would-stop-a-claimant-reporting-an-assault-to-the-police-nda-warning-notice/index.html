A firm acts for a gym operator defending a negligence claim by a member, Ms Tasha Greene, who was injured when a weights machine collapsed. Ms Greene also alleges that a member of staff assaulted her during the incident. The parties have agreed a settlement, and both are legally represented. The client wants the settlement agreement to include a clause that Ms Greene will not disclose the circumstances of the incident to anyone, 'including the police or any regulator', on pain of repaying the settlement sum.

Which of the following best describes whether the firm may include the clause?

Worked answer

Correct answer C: No: an NDA must not be used to prevent, impede or deter a person from reporting an offence to a law enforcement agency or making reports to a regulator, and a claw-back must not be used to achieve that effect.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

An NDA must not be used, including through claw-back clauses, to prevent or deter anyone from reporting an offence to the police or making reports to a regulator, even where all parties are represented.

Settlement NDA that would stop a claimant reporting an assault to the police (NDA warning notice)

The SRA's warning notice on non-disclosure agreements confirms that NDAs are not prohibited. However, it treats an NDA as improperly used if it is used to prevent, impede or deter a person from cooperating with a criminal investigation, reporting an offence to a law enforcement agency, or reporting to a regulator, among other protected disclosures. Claw-back clauses must not be used in a way designed to, or having the effect of, improperly inhibiting such reporting. The notice applies whatever the context of the NDA, including negligence disputes, and even where every party is legally represented. A clause barring Ms Greene from telling the police about an alleged assault, backed by a duty to repay the settlement, is squarely within the SRA's concern. Drafting it would risk breaching Principles 1, 2 and 5 and para 7.5 of the Code. The firm should advise the client accordingly and may include only a confidentiality clause that expressly preserves permitted disclosures. If the client insists on the improper clause, the firm must consider whether it can continue to act.

Why A is wrong: The SRA warning notice applies even if all parties to the NDA are legally represented.

Why B is wrong: Independent advice does not make it proper to use an NDA to stop a person reporting an offence or reporting to a regulator.

Why D is wrong: The warning notice applies to NDAs in any context, including negligence claims and commercial transactions.

Why E is wrong: The SRA does not prohibit NDAs; it prohibits their improper use, so a properly drafted confidentiality clause could be included.
