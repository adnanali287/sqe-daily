Order of payment from floating charge realisations in a liquidation (IA 1986 ss.175, 176A)

Ashgrove Interiors Ltd is in insolvent liquidation in England. Its bank holds a fixed charge over the company's warehouse and a floating charge over all its other assets, both created in 2019 and properly registered. The warehouse has been sold and the fixed charge proceeds paid to the bank. The liquidator has also realised a substantial sum from the floating charge assets. This sum is not enough to pay everyone in full, and the prescribed part has not been disapplied. The creditors include employees with preferential claims, unsecured trade creditors and the bank for the balance of its debt. The liquidator has also incurred expenses of the winding up.

In what order will the liquidator apply the floating charge realisations?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer A: Expenses of the winding up; preferential debts; the prescribed part set aside for unsecured creditors; the floating charge holder; any surplus to unsecured creditors and then to shareholders.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

Floating charge realisations go to winding-up expenses, then preferential debts, then the prescribed part is ring-fenced for unsecured creditors, before the floating chargeholder is paid (IA 1986 ss.175, 176A).

Order of payment from floating charge realisations in a liquidation (IA 1986 ss.175, 176A)

Under s.175(1) IA 1986, preferential debts are paid in priority to all other debts after the expenses of the winding up. Under s.175(2)(b), so far as the assets available to general creditors are insufficient, preferential debts have priority over the holder of a floating charge and are paid out of property subject to that charge. The floating charge proceeds are therefore applied first to the winding-up expenses and then to the preferential creditors. Next, s.176A(2) requires the liquidator to make a prescribed part of the company's net property available for unsecured debts. The liquidator must not distribute that part to the floating charge holder, except in so far as it exceeds what the unsecured debts need. Net property means the property that would otherwise be available to the floating charge holder (s.176A(6)). Only after the prescribed part is set aside does the bank receive the balance under its floating charge. Any surplus then goes to unsecured creditors and finally to shareholders.

Why B is wrong: A floating charge holder is postponed to the expenses and to preferential debts (s.175(1) and (2)(b)).

Why C is wrong: Expenses of the winding up are paid before preferential debts, and both come ahead of the floating charge holder.

Why D is wrong: Preferential debts rank ahead of the floating charge holder under s.175(2)(b), and the prescribed part is set aside before the chargeholder is paid.

Why E is wrong: The prescribed part must be set aside from net property before the floating charge holder is paid, not after (s.176A(2)).
