Lucinda died in 2026, domiciled in England, leaving all her shares in Lucinda Lettings Ltd to her son. She had owned all the issued shares for twelve years. The company is unquoted and its business consists entirely of owning and letting twelve residential flats on assured shorthold tenancies. Lucinda was a director and worked in the business full time.

Which of the following best describes the availability of business property relief (BPR) on the shares?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer D: BPR is not available, because the company's business consists wholly or mainly of making or holding investments.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

Shares in a company whose business is wholly or mainly dealing in land or securities or making or holding investments (such as property letting) are not relevant business property.

Business property relief unavailable for shares in an investment (property letting) company

Section 105(1) Inheritance Tax Act 1984 includes unquoted shares in a company within 'relevant business property'. However, s.105(3) provides that shares in a company are not relevant business property if the company's business 'consists wholly or mainly of one or more of the following, that is to say, dealing in securities, stocks or shares, land or buildings or making or holding investments'. Owning and letting residential flats is a business of making or holding investments, so the exclusion applies and Lucinda's shares do not qualify for BPR at any rate. The length of ownership, her control of the company and her personal involvement as a director cannot overcome the s.105(3) exclusion. The full value of the shares is therefore within her death estate for inheritance tax.

Why A is wrong: Unquoted status and the two-year ownership period are necessary, but they do not override the investment business exclusion in s.105(3).

Why B is wrong: Control is irrelevant where s.105(3) applies; the shares are not relevant business property at all.

Why C is wrong: Personal work in the business does not change the nature of the company's business, which is holding investments.

Why E is wrong: Unquoted shares, and controlling holdings of quoted shares, can be relevant business property under s.105(1); they fail here only because of s.105(3).
