Maya holds 60% of the shares in Fernhill Joinery Ltd, a private company limited by shares that has adopted the model articles without amendment. She wants to remove Tom, one of the company's three directors. Tom has a written service contract with the company that still has two years to run. Maya would prefer to avoid a meeting and asks whether she can simply circulate a written resolution to the shareholders.

Which of the following best describes how Tom can be removed as a director?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer C: By an ordinary resolution passed at a general meeting, special notice of the resolution having been given to the company at least 28 days before the meeting.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

A director can be removed by ordinary resolution at a general meeting on special notice (28 days to the company), and never by written resolution.

Removing a company director: ordinary resolution and special notice

Under s.168(1) Companies Act 2006 a company may remove a director by ordinary resolution at a meeting, notwithstanding anything in any agreement with the director. Special notice is required (s.168(2)), which means notice of the intention to move the resolution must be given to the company at least 28 days before the meeting (s.312(1)). A written resolution cannot be used for a s.168 removal (s.288(2)(a)), so a meeting is unavoidable.

Why A is wrong: s.288(2)(a) prohibits removing a director under s.168 by written resolution, and in any case only an ordinary (simple majority) resolution is needed.

Why B is wrong: an ordinary resolution suffices; a special resolution is not required.

Why D is wrong: the statutory power of removal belongs to the members; the model articles give the board no power to remove a fellow director.

Why E is wrong: s.168(1) applies notwithstanding any agreement; Tom's contract only preserves any claim to compensation or damages (s.168(5)).
