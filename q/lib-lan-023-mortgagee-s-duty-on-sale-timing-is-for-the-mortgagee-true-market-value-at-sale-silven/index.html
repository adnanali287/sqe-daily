Fenwick Lending Ltd holds a first legal charge over a block of commercial units in Middlesbrough, England, owned by Tanaka Developments Ltd, which has defaulted. Fenwick's power of sale has become exercisable. The local market is depressed, and Tanaka asks Fenwick to wait a year, when prices are expected to recover, and in the meantime to apply for planning permission for residential conversion, which would increase the value. Fenwick refuses, properly markets the units and sells them at the best price reasonably obtainable at that time.

Which of the following best describes Fenwick's liability to Tanaka?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer E: Fenwick is not liable, because a mortgagee may choose when to sell and need not improve the property, provided it takes reasonable care to obtain the true market value at the time it sells.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

A selling mortgagee chooses the timing and need not improve the property, but must act in good faith and take reasonable care to obtain the true market value at the time of sale.

Mortgagee's duty on sale: timing is for the mortgagee; true market value at sale (Silven)

A mortgagee exercising its power of sale owes the mortgagor a duty to take reasonable precautions to obtain the true market value or a proper price at the time when it chooses to sell, and must act in good faith. In Silven Properties Ltd v Royal Bank of Scotland plc [2003] EWCA Civ 1409 the Court of Appeal confirmed that the mortgagee is under no duty of care to the mortgagor as to the timing of a sale and can act in its own interests in deciding whether and when to sell. It also held that the mortgagee is entitled to sell the property as it is and is under no obligation to improve it or increase its value, for example by pursuing planning permission. Fenwick sold after proper marketing at the best price reasonably obtainable at the time, so it met its duty, and Tanaka has no claim even though a later sale might have produced more.

Why A is wrong: Silven confirms the mortgagee owes no duty as to the timing of a sale and may sell in a depressed market.

Why B is wrong: A mortgagee may sell the property as it is; it has no obligation to improve it or seek planning permission first.

Why C is wrong: A mortgagee is not a trustee of the power of sale; its duty is to take reasonable care to obtain the true market value at the time of sale.

Why D is wrong: The test is the true market value at the time of sale, not the mortgagor's own valuation.
