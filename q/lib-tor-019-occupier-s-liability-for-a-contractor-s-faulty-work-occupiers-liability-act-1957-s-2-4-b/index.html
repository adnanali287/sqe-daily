Elmhurst Leisure Ltd runs a gym in England. It hires Liftline Services Ltd, a well-established, properly accredited lift engineering company with good references, to repair the passenger lift. After the work, Elmhurst obtains Liftline's test certificate confirming the lift is safe. Unknown to Elmhurst, a Liftline engineer carelessly fitted a faulty cable, and nothing would have revealed this to a non-specialist. A week later the lift drops suddenly, injuring Priya, a gym member.

Which of the following best describes Elmhurst's likely liability to Priya under the Occupiers' Liability Act 1957?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer A: Elmhurst is unlikely to be liable, because it acted reasonably in entrusting technical work to a contractor and took reasonable steps to satisfy itself that the contractor was competent and the work properly done.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

An occupier is not answerable for a contractor's faulty work if it reasonably entrusted the work to the contractor and took reasonable steps to check the contractor's competence and the work.

Occupier's liability for a contractor's faulty work (Occupiers' Liability Act 1957 s.2(4)(b))

Section 2(4)(b) of the Occupiers' Liability Act 1957 provides that where damage is caused to a visitor by a danger due to the faulty execution of any work of construction, maintenance or repair by an independent contractor employed by the occupier, the occupier is not to be treated without more as answerable for the danger, if in all the circumstances it acted reasonably in entrusting the work to an independent contractor and took such steps (if any) as it reasonably ought to satisfy itself that the contractor was competent and that the work had been properly done. Lift repair is specialist work that it is reasonable to entrust to a contractor. Elmhurst chose an accredited, well-established firm with good references and obtained a test certificate; it could not be expected to inspect the cable itself. It has therefore discharged the common duty of care, and Priya's claim lies against Liftline, whose engineer was negligent.

Why B is wrong: Section 2(4)(b) allows an occupier to discharge its duty by reasonably entrusting work to a competent contractor and taking reasonable checks.

Why C is wrong: The common duty of care under the 1957 Act is a duty of reasonable care, not strict liability.

Why D is wrong: Priya was a lawful visitor, so the 1957 Act, not the 1984 Act, governs her claim.

Why E is wrong: A warning is not required here; the occupier is protected by having acted reasonably under s.2(4)(b), and a warning alone would not necessarily suffice anyway.
