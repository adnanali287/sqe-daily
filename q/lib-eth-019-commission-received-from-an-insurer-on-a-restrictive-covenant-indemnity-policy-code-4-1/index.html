Commission received from an insurer on a restrictive covenant indemnity policy (Code 4.1)

A firm acts for Leanne Choi on the sale of part of her land. The land is subject to an old restrictive covenant, and the buyer requires Ms Choi to provide a restrictive covenant indemnity insurance policy at her expense. The firm arranges the policy through a broker, and the insurer pays the firm £150 commission. The firm's client care letter says nothing about commissions.

What must the firm do with the £150 commission?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer B: Account to Ms Choi for it, unless she has agreed otherwise.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

A solicitor must account to the client for any financial benefit, such as a commission, received as a result of the client's instructions, unless the client has agreed otherwise.

Commission received from an insurer on a restrictive covenant indemnity policy (Code 4.1)

Paragraph 4.1 of the Code requires a solicitor to properly account to clients for any financial benefit received as a result of their instructions, except where the client has agreed otherwise. Paragraph 5.1 of the Code of Conduct for Firms imposes the same duty on the firm. The £150 commission is a financial benefit that the firm received only because it arranged the indemnity policy on Ms Choi's instructions and at her expense. It does not matter that the insurer, not Ms Choi, paid it. The firm must pay it to Ms Choi, for example by crediting it against her bill, unless she has agreed that the firm may keep it. Her agreement would need to be informed, so she would have to be told about the commission. The client care letter is silent and there is no such agreement, so the firm must account to her.

Why A is wrong: Para 4.1 covers any financial benefit received as a result of the client's instructions, whoever pays it.

Why C is wrong: Para 4.1 contains no de minimis threshold; the firm must account for any financial benefit unless the client has agreed otherwise.

Why D is wrong: The commission arose from Ms Choi's instructions and the policy was at her expense; the buyer is not the firm's client.

Why E is wrong: Showing it on a bill after the event is not the client agreeing that the firm may keep it.
