Daniel and Rosa Ferreira married in 2009. In March 2026 Daniel moved out of the family home in England to live with a new partner, and the couple agreed to separate. At a meeting a week later, after long discussion, Daniel signed a note: "In return for you paying off the remaining £4,000 loan on the family car, I will pay you £600 a month towards your living costs." Rosa paid off the car loan from her own earnings. Daniel paid nothing and now says the note was a family arrangement with no legal effect.

Which of the following best describes whether the agreement is legally binding?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer E: It is binding, because the presumption against legal relations in domestic arrangements does not apply where spouses have separated or are about to separate and bargain at arm's length.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

The presumption that domestic agreements are not intended to be binding does not apply where spouses are separated or separating; their arm's-length agreements are presumed to be intended to create legal relations.

Intention to create legal relations between spouses who have separated (Merritt v Merritt)

Agreements between spouses living together in amity are ordinarily not intended to create legal relations (Balfour v Balfour). In Merritt v Merritt the Court of Appeal held that this is altogether different where the parties are separated or about to separate: they then bargain keenly and do not rely on honourable understandings, and Lord Denning MR said it may safely be presumed that they intend to create legal relations. The court asks objectively whether reasonable people would regard the agreement as intended to be binding, rather than looking into the parties' minds. Daniel and Rosa had separated, discussed the arrangement at length and recorded it in a signed note. Rosa provided consideration by paying off the car loan. The agreement is therefore legally binding.

Why A is wrong: Merritt v Merritt holds that the domestic presumption does not apply once spouses have separated or are about to separate.

Why B is wrong: Rosa's payment of the loan, which Daniel requested in return for his promise, is good consideration, as the wife's mortgage payments were in Merritt.

Why C is wrong: There is no rule that contracts between spouses must be made by deed; Merritt enforced a signed note.

Why D is wrong: Intention is judged objectively by what reasonable people would conclude, not by the promisor's private state of mind.
