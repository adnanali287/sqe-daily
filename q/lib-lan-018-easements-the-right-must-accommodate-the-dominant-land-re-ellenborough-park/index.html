Nell owns a freehold café beside a lake in Cumbria, England. The adjoining landowner, Victor, who owns the lake, has offered to grant Nell and her successors a number of rights by deed. Nell wants to know which proposed rights would be capable of existing as easements benefiting the café land.

Which of the following proposed rights is LEAST likely to be capable of existing as an easement?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer B: An exclusive right to run a business hiring out rowing boats to the public on Victor's lake.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

A right is an easement only if it accommodates the dominant land, being connected with its normal enjoyment; a commercial monopoly unrelated to the land's use, as in Hill v Tupper, does not qualify.

Easements: the right must accommodate the dominant land (Re Ellenborough Park)

In Re Ellenborough Park [1955] EWCA Civ 4 the Court of Appeal adopted four characteristics of an easement: there must be a dominant and a servient tenement; the easement must accommodate the dominant tenement; the dominant and servient owners must be different persons; and the right must be capable of forming the subject-matter of a grant. On accommodation, the right must be connected with the normal enjoyment of the dominant land, not merely make its owner richer. The Court explained Hill v Tupper, where an exclusive right to let out boats on a canal failed as an easement because it was an independent business monopoly: the land was merely a convenient incident to the right. A boat-hire monopoly on Victor's lake is the same: it does not serve the café land as land. Rights of way, drainage, access paths and light are recognised types of right that serve the dominant land.

Why A is wrong: A right of way to reach the dominant land plainly accommodates it and is a well-recognised easement.

Why C is wrong: Rights to run pipes and drainage for the dominant land are well-recognised easements.

Why D is wrong: A defined path giving access to the dominant land serves its normal use and is capable of being an easement.

Why E is wrong: A right to light for the windows of a building on the dominant land is a long-recognised easement that serves the land.
