Hugo owns a cottage and the neighbouring paddock in Dorset, England, both registered under one title. In 2019 he let the cottage to Lena on a periodic tenancy. The cottage has its own frontage to a lane, but Hugo told Lena informally that she could also drive across the paddock along a gravel track to reach the main road, and she has done so most days since. In 2026 Hugo sold and transferred the freehold of the cottage to Lena, keeping the paddock. The transfer says nothing about the track and does not exclude s.62 of the Law of Property Act 1925. Hugo now wants to stop Lena using the track.

Which of the following best describes Lena's position?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer E: She has a legal easement, because the transfer operated under s.62 to convey with the cottage the right of way that was enjoyed with it at the time of the conveyance.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

On a conveyance, s.62 LPA 1925 passes rights enjoyed with the land at that time, including a tenant's permissive use of a way over the seller's retained land, converting it into a legal easement.

Section 62 LPA 1925: a tenant's permissive right becomes an easement on sale of the freehold

Section 62(1) LPA 1925 provides that a conveyance of land operates to convey with it all easements, rights and advantages appertaining to the land or, at the time of conveyance, enjoyed with it, unless a contrary intention is expressed (s.62(4)). In Kent v Kavanagh [2006] EWCA Civ 162 the Court of Appeal held that these words are apt to convey, with the freehold, rights of way over the retained land that are, at the time of the conveyance, enjoyed by the tenant in occupation of the land conveyed. Before the sale, Lena occupied the cottage and Hugo occupied the paddock, so there was diversity of occupation, and she was in fact enjoying the right of way. The informal and revocable nature of the permission does not prevent s.62 operating; this is the long-established effect of the section. The transfer therefore converted the permission into an easement. An easement created by s.62 is not an express grant requiring registration, so it is legal.

Why A is wrong: Section 62 can convert a precarious permission enjoyed with the land into an easement on a conveyance; revocability is no bar.

Why B is wrong: Necessity is a separate route; s.62 does not require the land to be landlocked.

Why C is wrong: Kent v Kavanagh treats rights enjoyed by a tenant as passing under s.62, rather than artificially treating the landlord as using them under Wheeldon v Burrows.

Why D is wrong: Prescription is unnecessary where s.62 has already operated, and use by permission would not count as use as of right.
