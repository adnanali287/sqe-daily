Bounce Zone Ltd runs a trampoline park in England. Every customer pays for entry and must tick a box online confirming they have read a notice which says: 'Bounce Zone accepts no liability for any injury, however caused, including injury caused by our negligence.' The same notice is displayed at the entrance. Hannah, a customer, is injured when a trampoline collapses because Bounce Zone's staff negligently failed to replace a worn spring they had been told about.

Which of the following best describes the effect of the notice on Hannah's claim for her injury?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer B: The notice cannot exclude Bounce Zone's liability for personal injury resulting from negligence, and Hannah is not taken to have accepted the risk merely because she knew of it.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

A trader cannot by a consumer contract term or consumer notice exclude or restrict liability for death or personal injury resulting from negligence (CRA 2015 s.65).

Excluding liability for personal injury to a consumer visitor (Consumer Rights Act 2015 s.65)

Section 65(1) of the Consumer Rights Act 2015 provides that a trader cannot by a term of a consumer contract or by a consumer notice exclude or restrict liability for death or personal injury resulting from negligence. 'Negligence' includes breach of the common duty of care under the Occupiers' Liability Act 1957 (s.65(4)(c)). Hannah is a consumer paying to use the trampoline park, and Bounce Zone is a trader. Her injury resulted from its staff's negligence, so the notice is of no effect as regards her personal injury, however prominently it was displayed and whether or not she agreed to it. Section 65(2) adds that a person is not to be taken to have voluntarily accepted any risk merely because she agreed to or knew about the term or notice, so Bounce Zone cannot use volenti to achieve the same result. The recreational access exception in s.66(4) does not apply, because allowing access was within the purposes of Bounce Zone's business.

Why A is wrong: Agreement to the notice does not make it effective: s.65(1) bars exclusion of negligence liability for personal injury, and s.65(2) prevents implied consent.

Why C is wrong: There is no reasonableness test for personal injury caused by negligence; exclusion is simply prohibited.

Why D is wrong: Prominence and incorporation are irrelevant where the exclusion is prohibited by statute.

Why E is wrong: The bar applies to all negligence, whether inadvertent or intentional (s.65(5)); the claimant need not prove recklessness.
