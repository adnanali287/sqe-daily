Who is liable for a defective product under the Consumer Protection Act 1987 s.2

Hartwell Supermarkets plc sells electric kettles under its own brand, 'Hartwell Home', with no other maker's name on them. The kettles are made in Malaysia by Sunlit Appliances Sdn Bhd and imported into the United Kingdom by Corran Imports Ltd, which sells them to Hartwell in the course of its business. Zara buys one from a Hartwell store in England. A defect in the kettle causes it to split, and Zara is scalded. She wants to claim under Part I of the Consumer Protection Act 1987.

Which of the following best describes who is liable to Zara under Part I of the Act?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer E: Sunlit Appliances, Hartwell and Corran Imports, each of whom is jointly and severally liable.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

Under CPA 1987 s.2, the producer, an own-brander holding itself out as producer, and the importer into the UK are each liable, jointly and severally, for damage caused by a defective product.

Who is liable for a defective product under the Consumer Protection Act 1987 s.2

Section 2(1) of the Consumer Protection Act 1987 makes every person to whom s.2(2) applies liable for damage caused wholly or partly by a defect in a product. Section 2(2) applies to (a) the producer of the product, which includes Sunlit as manufacturer; (b) any person who, by putting its name on the product or using a trade mark or other distinguishing mark in relation to it, has held itself out to be the producer, which covers Hartwell by selling the kettle as its own brand with no other maker's name; and (c) any person who imported the product into the United Kingdom in order, in the course of its business, to supply it to another, which covers Corran. Under s.2(5), where two or more persons are liable for the same damage, their liability is joint and several, so Zara can recover her loss from any of them. A mere supplier is liable only under s.2(3), but Hartwell is liable here as an own-brander.

Why A is wrong: Section 2(2) extends liability beyond the manufacturer to own-branders and importers into the UK.

Why B is wrong: Hartwell is liable because it held itself out as producer, but it is not the only person liable.

Why C is wrong: The importer is liable in addition to, not instead of, the manufacturer and the own-brander.

Why D is wrong: Hartwell is not a mere supplier: by selling the kettle under its own brand it held itself out as the producer under s.2(2)(b).
