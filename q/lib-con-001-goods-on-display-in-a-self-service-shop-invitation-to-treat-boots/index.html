Imani Okafor, an adult, takes a cordless drill from a shelf in a DIY store in England. The shelf label wrongly shows the price as £19 instead of £119. She puts the drill in her basket and takes it to the till. The cashier notices the error and says the store will only sell the drill for £119. Imani insists that a contract was made at £19 when she placed the drill in her basket.

Which of the following best describes the legal position?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer B: No contract has been made: the shelf display was an invitation to treat, Imani made an offer at the till, and the store is free to reject it.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

Goods displayed with prices in a shop are an invitation to treat; the customer offers to buy at the till and the shop may accept or reject that offer.

Goods on display in a self-service shop: invitation to treat (Boots)

In Pharmaceutical Society of Great Britain v Boots Cash Chemists (Southern) Ltd the Court of Appeal held that goods displayed with prices in a self-service shop are not an offer by the shopkeeper. The display invites customers to choose goods and bring them to the cashier. The customer then makes the offer to buy, and the contract is concluded only when the shopkeeper, or someone on its behalf, accepts that offer. One reason the court gave was that otherwise a customer who put an item in the basket could never put it back. Applying this, the shelf label was an invitation to treat. Imani's act of placing the drill in her basket did not accept anything. She made an offer at the till, and the cashier's refusal to sell at £19 was a rejection of that offer. No contract exists at £19.

Why A is wrong: Boots holds that placing goods in a basket is not acceptance; the customer remains free to put the item back.

Why C is wrong: A price label on goods in a shop is an invitation to treat, not a unilateral offer capable of acceptance by reading it.

Why D is wrong: Acceptance is the shop's agreement to the customer's offer; the cashier rejected the £19 offer, so there was no acceptance at that price.

Why E is wrong: Contract law imposes no obligation on a shop to sell at a displayed price; any pricing rules are regulatory, not a source of a contract.
