Oluwaseun Adebayo was injured when a car hit him while he was cycling in Nottingham. He consults Harrow Penney, an SRA-authorised firm, about a claim against the driver which he values at around £25,000. At the first meeting, the solicitor discusses how the claim might be funded.

Which step best reflects proper practice for the solicitor when considering funding at the outset?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer D: Ask Oluwaseun to produce his motor, household and any stand-alone legal expenses insurance policies, to check whether he already has before-the-event cover for the claim.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

When advising on funding, a solicitor should normally ask the client to produce motor, household and any stand-alone BTE policies to check for legal expenses insurance (Sarwar v Alam [2001] EWCA Civ 1401).

Legal expenses insurance: checking for before-the-event cover at the outset (Sarwar v Alam)

In Sarwar v Alam [2001] EWCA Civ 1401 the Court of Appeal said that proper modern practice dictates that a solicitor should normally invite a client to bring to the first interview any relevant motor insurance policy, household insurance policy and any stand-alone before-the-event (BTE) insurance policy. Such policies often include legal expenses insurance that may fund the claim. Checking for BTE cover is therefore an essential first step when advising on funding, alongside other options such as a conditional fee agreement, a damages-based agreement or a private retainer. The court stressed that the enquiry should be proportionate and that the guidance is not an inflexible code. Here, asking Oluwaseun to produce his policies is the step that best reflects that practice.

Why A is wrong: Legal expenses (BTE) insurance commonly covers personal injury claims, and its availability should be checked before other arrangements are made.

Why B is wrong: Personal injury claims are excluded from civil legal aid under LASPO 2012 Sch 1 Pt 2 para 1.

Why C is wrong: This ignores other options, including conditional fee agreements and legal expenses insurance.

Why E is wrong: Concern about choice of solicitor does not remove the need to check whether BTE cover exists; the solicitor should still enquire.
