Amira Haddad, aged 26, appears before a magistrates' court in England charged with theft from her employer, an either-way offence. The charge is read to her and the court explains the plea before venue procedure. She indicates that she would plead guilty.

What is the effect of Amira's indication?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer C: The court proceeds as if Amira has pleaded guilty at a summary trial; it may sentence her or, if it considers the offence so serious that the Crown Court should have power to deal with her, commit her to the Crown Court for sentence.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

A guilty plea indication at plea before venue is treated as a guilty plea at summary trial; the magistrates sentence or commit to the Crown Court for sentence.

Effect of indicating a guilty plea at plea before venue (MCA 1980 s.17A)

Section 17A of the Magistrates' Courts Act 1980 governs plea before venue for adults charged with either-way offences. The court must explain that, if the accused indicates a guilty plea, the court will proceed as described in s.17A(6) and the accused may be committed to the Crown Court for sentence (s.17A(4)). Under s.17A(6), where the accused indicates a guilty plea, the court proceeds as if the proceedings had been a summary trial from the beginning and she had pleaded guilty. Amira therefore stands convicted. No allocation decision is made and there is no right to elect jury trial. The magistrates may sentence her themselves or, under s.14 of the Sentencing Code, commit her for sentence if they consider the offence so serious that the Crown Court should have the power to deal with her.

Why A is wrong: Allocation under ss.19-20 follows only an indication of not guilty (s.17A(7)); a guilty indication bypasses it.

Why B is wrong: Section 17A(6) treats a guilty indication as a guilty plea at summary trial, so she is convicted.

Why D is wrong: The case stays in the magistrates' court; the Crown Court is involved only if the court commits her for sentence.

Why E is wrong: Once she has indicated a guilty plea she is treated as convicted on summary trial, so there is no election.
