Delroy Campbell is a sole practitioner whose SRA-authorised firm in England has qualifying professional indemnity insurance on the SRA Minimum Terms and Conditions (MTC). He retires and closes the practice at the end of the policy period. No other firm takes over the practice, so there is no successor practice. Two years later a former client, Martina Kovač, discovers that Delroy made a negligent mistake in her property transaction shortly before he retired.

Which of the following best describes the indemnity insurance position for Martina's claim?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer E: The insurer must provide run-off cover extending the period of insurance for an additional six years, so a claim made two years after closure is covered.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

On cessation of a practice with no successor, qualifying insurance must provide run-off cover extending the period of insurance for an additional six years (SRA MTC clauses 5.3-5.4).

Closing a firm with no successor: six years' run-off cover under the SRA Minimum Terms

The MTC in the SRA Indemnity Insurance Rules require the insurance to provide run-off cover in the event of a cessation of the practice (clause 5.3). Clause 5.4(d) provides that run-off cover must extend the period of insurance for an additional six years, ending on the sixth anniversary of the date on which it would otherwise have ended. Because Delroy's practice ceased with no successor practice, his insurer must provide that run-off cover. A claim by Martina made two years after closure falls within the six-year run-off period. The run-off cover must also provide the minimum level of cover required by clause 2, so she has an insured claim against the firm for Delroy's negligence.

Why A is wrong: The MTC require run-off cover on cessation precisely so that claims about past work remain insured after closure.

Why B is wrong: The Compensation Fund is a discretionary fund of last resort; it does not replace the run-off cover the MTC require.

Why C is wrong: The extended policy period and cessation period are separate, shorter periods; run-off cover runs for a further six years.

Why D is wrong: The obligation falls on the existing insurer to provide run-off cover; a retired sole practitioner does not need new annual policies for past work.
