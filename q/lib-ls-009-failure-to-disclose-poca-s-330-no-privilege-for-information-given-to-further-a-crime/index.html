Failure to disclose (POCA s.330): no privilege for information given to further a crime

Joanna Mazur, a solicitor at an SRA-authorised firm in Bristol, is acting for Kofi Mensah on his purchase of a house, a matter within the regulated sector. In a meeting at which she is advising him on the purchase, Kofi tells her that the deposit and the balance of the price will come from cash his brother made selling stolen goods, and asks her to complete quickly so the money is 'turned into bricks' before anyone asks questions. Joanna is the firm's fee-earner, not its nominated officer.

What is Joanna's obligation under s.330 of the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer E: She must make a disclosure to the firm's nominated officer as soon as is practicable, because information communicated with the intention of furthering a criminal purpose does not come to her in privileged circumstances.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

A regulated-sector legal adviser who suspects money laundering must disclose to the nominated officer as soon as practicable; privilege does not protect information given to further a crime (POCA s.330).

Failure to disclose (POCA s.330): no privilege for information given to further a crime

Section 330 makes it an offence for a person in the regulated sector who knows or suspects, or has reasonable grounds for knowing or suspecting, that another person is engaged in money laundering not to make the required disclosure to a nominated officer (or the NCA) as soon as is practicable. Joanna has at least suspicion and can identify the persons involved. Section 330(6)(b) and (10) give a professional legal adviser an exception where the information came to her in privileged circumstances, such as from a client in connection with legal advice. But s.330(11) disapplies that where the information is communicated with the intention of furthering a criminal purpose. Kofi told her about the source of funds so that she would complete the purchase and launder the money, so the exception is unavailable and she must report to the nominated officer.

Why A is wrong: Section 330(11) removes the privileged-circumstances protection where information is given with the intention of furthering a criminal purpose, as here.

Why B is wrong: The s.330 test includes suspicion and reasonable grounds for knowing or suspecting, not only actual knowledge.

Why C is wrong: The disclosure must be made as soon as is practicable; completing first would also risk a principal money laundering offence.

Why D is wrong: Telling Kofi about a report is likely to prejudice an investigation and risks the tipping-off offence; there is no duty to warn him.
