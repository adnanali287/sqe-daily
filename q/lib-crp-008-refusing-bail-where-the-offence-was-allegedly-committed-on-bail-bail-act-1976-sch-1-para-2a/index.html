Callum Fraser, aged 34, appears before a magistrates' court in England charged with burglary of a dwelling, an either-way offence. The burglary is alleged to have been committed while he was on bail awaiting trial for an unrelated theft. The prosecution opposes bail. The court is satisfied that, if convicted, there is a real prospect that Callum will receive an immediate custodial sentence.

Which of the following best describes the court's power in relation to bail?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer A: The court need not grant bail, because the offence is triable either way and it appears that Callum was on bail in criminal proceedings on the date of the offence.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

A court need not grant bail to a defendant charged with an indictable or either-way offence who appears to have been on bail in criminal proceedings when it was committed.

Refusing bail where the offence was allegedly committed on bail (Bail Act 1976 Sch 1 para 2A)

Under s.4 of the Bail Act 1976 a defendant has a general right to bail, subject to the exceptions in Schedule 1. Part 1 of Schedule 1 applies to imprisonable offences. Paragraph 2A provides that the defendant need not be granted bail if the offence is an indictable or either-way offence and it appears to the court that he was on bail in criminal proceedings on the date of the offence. Burglary of a dwelling is triable either way, and Callum was on bail for the theft when it is alleged to have been committed. The court therefore has a discretion to refuse bail on this ground alone. Because the court considers there is a real prospect of immediate custody, the 'no real prospect of custody' restriction in para 1A does not prevent reliance on para 2A. The wording is permissive: refusal is not compulsory.

Why B is wrong: Paragraph 2A says the defendant 'need not' be granted bail; it creates a discretion, not a duty to refuse.

Why C is wrong: There is no rule that bail must be granted absent a previous failure to surrender; para 2A is an independent exception.

Why D is wrong: Paragraph 2A turns on the current offence being indictable or either-way, not on the nature of the earlier proceedings.

Why E is wrong: Offending while on bail is a separate statutory exception in para 2A, quite apart from the para 2 grounds.
