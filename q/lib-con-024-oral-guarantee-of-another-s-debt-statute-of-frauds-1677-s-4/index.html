Kestrel Packaging Ltd supplies boxes on credit to Mill Lane Bakery Ltd. When Mill Lane fell behind with payments, Kestrel threatened to stop supplying. Mill Lane's managing director, Ahmed Siddiqui, told Kestrel's sales manager in a phone call: "Keep supplying us. If the company doesn't pay you, I personally will." Nothing was written down or signed. Kestrel kept supplying, Mill Lane has now gone into liquidation owing £18,000, and Kestrel sues Ahmed personally.

Which of the following best describes whether Kestrel can enforce Ahmed's promise against him?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer C: No, because a promise to answer for the debt of another is a guarantee, which cannot be enforced unless it, or a note of it, is in writing signed by Ahmed or his authorised agent.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

A guarantee (a promise to answer for another's debt, default or miscarriage) is unenforceable unless it, or a note of it, is in writing signed by the guarantor or his authorised agent (Statute of Frauds 1677 s.4).

Oral guarantee of another's debt (Statute of Frauds 1677 s.4)

Section 4 of the Statute of Frauds 1677 provides that no action shall be brought to charge a defendant upon any special promise to answer for the debt, default or miscarriage of another person unless the agreement, or some memorandum or note of it, is in writing and signed by the party to be charged or by someone lawfully authorised by him. Ahmed's promise was that he would pay if the company did not. That is a guarantee: a secondary promise to answer for Mill Lane's debt, which remained the company's own primary liability. Nothing was written or signed, so Kestrel cannot bring an action on the promise against Ahmed, however clear the evidence of what he said. The formality requirement applies even though Ahmed, as a director, had an interest in the company continuing to receive supplies.

Why A is wrong: The general rule that contracts need no form is subject to statutory exceptions, and s.4 is one of them for guarantees.

Why B is wrong: A personal interest in the debtor's business does not take a promise to answer for the company's debt outside s.4.

Why D is wrong: Section 4 requires a signed writing or note, not a deed; a signed guarantee under hand would be enforceable.

Why E is wrong: Oral evidence of the promise does not satisfy s.4, which requires a written agreement or note signed by the guarantor or his agent.
