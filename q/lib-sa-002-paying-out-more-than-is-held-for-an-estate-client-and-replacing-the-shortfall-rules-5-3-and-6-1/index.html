Bryn & Adeyemi Solicitors acts for the executors of the late Mr Lindqvist. The client ledger for the estate shows £2,000 held in client account. At the fee earner's request, the cashier pays £3,500 from the general client account to settle an estate liability, because the fee earner expects £20,000 from the deceased's bank to arrive next week. The payment has been made. The firm's COFA discovers it the same day.

Which of the following best describes what the firm must now do?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer D: Immediately transfer £1,500 of the firm's own money from business account into client account to replace the shortfall.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

Client money may be withdrawn only if enough is held for that client; any shortfall caused by a withdrawal must be replaced immediately from the firm's own money.

Paying out more than is held for an estate client and replacing the shortfall (rules 5.3 and 6.1)

Rule 5.3 of the SRA Accounts Rules permits a withdrawal of client money only if sufficient funds are held on behalf of that specific client to make the payment. Only £2,000 was held for the estate, so paying £3,500 used £1,500 belonging to other clients, which breaches rule 5.3. Rule 6.1 requires breaches to be corrected promptly on discovery, and 'any money improperly withheld or withdrawn from a client account must be immediately paid into the account or replaced as appropriate'. The firm must therefore transfer £1,500 of its own money from business account to client account at once, so that other clients' money is restored. Expected receipts do not excuse the breach. The firm should also consider whether the breach must be reported to the SRA under its regulatory reporting duties.

Why A is wrong: Rule 6.1 requires immediate replacement; waiting for expected estate funds leaves other clients' money used for this client.

Why B is wrong: The firm must replace the shortfall immediately from its own money and record the transactions contemporaneously (rule 8.1); it cannot wait for the client.

Why C is wrong: The accountant's report does not correct a breach; rule 6.1 requires prompt correction on discovery.

Why E is wrong: Only the £1,500 shortfall needs replacing; the £2,000 held for the estate was properly used, and the liability was a genuine estate debt.
