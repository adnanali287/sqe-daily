Gethin has exchanged contracts to sell his freehold smallholding in Herefordshire, England, to Laila. The contract says nothing about a timber cabin in the orchard. The cabin was built on site about 30 years ago from timber frame panels and feather boarding. It rests by its own weight on concrete blocks set in the ground and is not bolted down. It has been used as a holiday home, and it cannot be taken down and rebuilt elsewhere: it could be removed only by demolishing it. Before completion, Gethin tells Laila that the cabin is his personal property and that he intends to sell it separately.

Which of the following best describes whether the cabin passes to Laila on completion?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer D: It passes, because the degree and object of annexation show it became part of the land: it was built to stay on site and cannot be removed except by demolition.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

An object becomes part of the land according to the degree and object of annexation, judged objectively; a structure that can be removed only by demolition is part of the land.

Chattel or part of the land: degree and object of annexation (Elitestone v Morris)

Whether an object brought onto land has become part of it depends mainly on the degree of annexation and the object (purpose) of annexation, assessed objectively. In Elitestone Ltd v Morris [1997] UKHL 15 a bungalow resting by its own weight on concrete pillars was held to be part of the land. Lord Lloyd said that a house constructed so that it cannot be removed at all, save by destruction, cannot have been intended to remain a chattel. The House also held that the subjective intention of the parties is relevant only so far as it can be derived from the degree and object of annexation. Gethin's cabin was built on site as a dwelling, has stood for decades, and could be removed only by demolition. It is therefore part of the land and passes to Laila with the freehold, whatever Gethin now believes.

Why A is wrong: Absence of physical attachment is not decisive; in Elitestone a building resting by its own weight became part of the land.

Why B is wrong: Subjective intention is irrelevant except as shown by the degree and object of annexation.

Why C is wrong: Anything forming part of the land passes with it unless excluded; it need not be listed in the contract.

Why E is wrong: Service connections are not required; the bungalow in Elitestone was treated as not physically attached yet still formed part of the land.
