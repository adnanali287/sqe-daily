Oluwaseun Bakare, aged 41, is charged with an either-way offence before a magistrates' court in England. He indicates a not guilty plea, and the court decides that the case is more suitable for summary trial. He asks for an indication of sentence, and the court indicates that a non-custodial sentence would be more likely if he were tried summarily and pleaded guilty. He then indicates that he would plead guilty. At the sentencing hearing, a differently constituted bench considers that the offence deserves a custodial sentence.

Which of the following best describes the court's position?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer E: No court may impose a custodial sentence for the offence, because custody was not indicated.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

If an accused pleads guilty after a non-custodial indication of sentence under s.20, no court may impose a custodial sentence for that offence.

Binding effect of a non-custodial indication of sentence (MCA 1980 ss.20 and 20A)

Under s.20 of the Magistrates' Courts Act 1980, once the court has decided that summary trial is more suitable, the accused may request an indication of whether a custodial or non-custodial sentence would be more likely if he were tried summarily and pleaded guilty (s.20(3)). If an indication is given and the accused then indicates a guilty plea, the court proceeds as if he had pleaded guilty at summary trial (s.20(7)). Section 20A(1) then provides that, where the case is dealt with under s.20(7), no court, whether a magistrates' court or not, may impose a custodial sentence for the offence unless such a sentence was indicated. Because the indication was non-custodial, no bench, however constituted, may impose custody. Section 20A(3) makes indications otherwise non-binding, but the protection in s.20A(1) applies here.

Why A is wrong: Section 20A(1) makes a non-custodial indication binding where the accused then pleads guilty under s.20(7).

Why B is wrong: Section 20A(1) binds 'no court (whether a magistrates' court or not)', not just the bench that gave the indication.

Why C is wrong: Credit for the plea does not unlock custody; s.20A(1) prohibits a custodial sentence altogether unless indicated.

Why D is wrong: The Act gives no route to impose custody by allowing withdrawal of the plea; s.20A(1) simply bars custody.
