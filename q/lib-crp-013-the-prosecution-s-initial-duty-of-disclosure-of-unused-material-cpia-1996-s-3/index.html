The prosecution's initial duty of disclosure of unused material (CPIA 1996 s.3)

Shauna Clarke is charged with robbery and pleads not guilty. Among the material gathered by the police, which the prosecution does not intend to use, is a statement from a passer-by describing the robber as having a full beard. Shauna's appearance at the time does not match that description, and the issue at trial is identification.

What must the prosecutor do with the passer-by's statement?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer A: Disclose it to the defence, because it might reasonably be considered capable of undermining the prosecution case or assisting Shauna's case.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

The prosecutor must disclose any unused material that might reasonably be considered capable of undermining the prosecution case or assisting the accused's case.

The prosecution's initial duty of disclosure of unused material (CPIA 1996 s.3)

Section 3(1) of the Criminal Procedure and Investigations Act 1996 imposes the prosecutor's initial duty of disclosure. The prosecutor must disclose to the accused any prosecution material not previously disclosed which might reasonably be considered capable of undermining the case for the prosecution or of assisting the case for the accused, or give a written statement that there is no such material. The test is objective and applies to unused material, that is, material the prosecution does not intend to rely on. A witness description of the robber as bearded, when Shauna did not match it, plainly might be considered capable of undermining the identification case and assisting the defence. It must therefore be disclosed at the initial stage, without waiting for a defence statement or a court order.

Why B is wrong: The s.3 duty arises first; a defence statement comes later and triggers the continuing duty, not the initial one.

Why C is wrong: Unused material is precisely what s.3 is aimed at; the prosecution's intention not to rely on it is irrelevant.

Why D is wrong: The s.3 duty is a statutory obligation on the prosecutor and needs no court order.

Why E is wrong: Section 3 requires disclosure only of material meeting the undermine-or-assist test, not all unused material.
