Hamza died intestate in 2026, domiciled in England, leaving his wife, Nadia, and their son, Idris, aged 9. After debts, the estate (excluding personal chattels) is worth £600,000, which exceeds the statutory legacy. Nadia wishes to apply for the grant of letters of administration on her own, and no one else in the family is keen to be involved.

Which of the following best describes the position on the grant?

Choose one answer to see the reasoning.

Worked answer

Correct answer E: Because a minority interest arises, the grant must be made to at least two individuals (or a trust corporation) unless the court considers a sole administrator expedient.

THE RULE TO REMEMBER

Where a minor beneficiary or life interest arises, letters of administration must issue to at least two individuals or a trust corporation, unless the court allows a sole administrator.

Minority interest on intestacy: two administrators required (Senior Courts Act 1981 s.114(2))

Nadia has first priority to a grant under r.22 Non-Contentious Probate Rules 1987 as the surviving spouse. However, the estate exceeds the personal chattels plus the statutory legacy, so under s.46 Administration of Estates Act 1925 part of the residue is held on the statutory trusts for Idris, who is 9. That gives rise to a minority interest. Section 114(2) Senior Courts Act 1981 provides that where under a will or intestacy any beneficiary is a minor or a life interest arises, any grant of administration must be made to a trust corporation (with or without an individual) or to 'not less than two individuals', unless it appears to the court expedient in all the circumstances to appoint a sole administrator. Nadia should therefore apply together with a second administrator. If the estate had not exceeded the chattels and statutory legacy, Idris would take nothing and no minority interest would arise.

Why A is wrong: Priority under r.22 does not override s.114(2), which requires at least two administrators where a minority interest arises.

Why B is wrong: A minority interest does not disqualify the spouse; it only requires a second administrator or a trust corporation.

Why C is wrong: A minor cannot take a grant, and the requirement is for a second administrator, not for the minor beneficiary to apply.

Why D is wrong: Four is the maximum number under s.114(1), not a minimum; two individuals suffice.
