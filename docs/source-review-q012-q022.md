## q012: Saunders v Vautier (Trusts)
- Old link: Trustee Act 2000 Explanatory Notes, para 120 (official, but secondary; it restates the rule while discussing s.34).
- New link: https://www.bailii.org/ew/cases/EWHC/Ch/1841/J82.html (judgment text, BAILII). No official government copy of an 1841 judgment exists (National Archives Find Case Law starts in 2001), so BAILII is the best freely available primary source.
- Support found:
  - The will directed accumulation "until Daniel Wright Vautier ... should attain his age of twenty-five years, and then to pay or transfer the principal ... unto the said Daniel Wright Vautier ... absolutely" (no gift over).
  - Lord Cottenham LC: "There is no gift over ... I am clearly of opinion that he is entitled to it." and "The order for a transfer of the funds, upon the regular evidence of the legatee having attained twenty-one, will follow".
  - This supports the answer (C): an absolutely entitled adult beneficiary can call for the fund despite a postponement of enjoyment. It also supports the D explanation that 21 was full age at the time.
- Additional official check (not linked): Family Law Reform Act 1969 s.1(1) (legislation.gov.uk): a person attains full age at eighteen instead of twenty-one. This supports option D being wrong.
- Note: BAILII carries the Lord Chancellor's report (Cr & Ph 240; 41 ER 482); the cited authority (4 Beav 115; 49 ER 282) is the Master of the Rolls' report of the same case. BAILII lists both citations. The authority line is unchanged.

## q022: Notice to complete (Property Practice)
- Old link: copy of the SCS hosted on a law firm's client portal.
- New link: https://www.lawsociety.org.uk/-/media/files/topics/property/specimen-standard-conditions-of-sale-residential-property-2018.pdf. This is the Law Society's own specimen of the Standard Conditions of Sale (Fifth Edition – 2018 Revision), linked from https://www.lawsociety.org.uk/topics/property/standard-conditions-of-sale (the page was updated 12 Nov 2024). The SCS are a contractual standard form, not legislation; the Law Society is their publisher.
- Support found (quoted from the PDF):
  - 1.3.2: "Giving a notice or delivering a document to a party's conveyancer has the same effect as giving or delivering it to that party."
  - 6.1.1: "Completion date is twenty working days after the date of the contract but time is not of the essence of the contract unless a notice to complete has been served."
  - 6.8.1: "At any time after the time applicable under condition 6.1.2 on completion date, a party who is ready, able and willing to complete may give the other a notice to complete."
  - 6.8.2: "The parties are to complete the contract within ten working days of giving a notice to complete, excluding the day on which the notice is given. For this purpose, time is of the essence of the contract."
  - 6.8.3(b): "if the buyer paid a deposit of less than 10 per cent, he is forthwith to pay a further deposit equal to the balance of that 10 per cent."
  - 7.4.1/7.4.2: if the buyer fails to complete in accordance with a notice to complete, "The seller may rescind the contract, and if he does so: (a) he may: (i) forfeit and keep any deposit and accrued interest (ii) resell the property ... (iii) claim damages".
- Every statement in the answer, the explanation and the key rule matches the Law Society text. No wording changes were needed.
